TF-IDF: Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency
● Classical technique in textual information retrieval
● Based on exact lexical matching, oblivious to word ordering (“bag of words”)
● Still widely used, even in the age of LLMs
○ Exact word matches are still a desideratum in many use cases
○ Supports very efficient implementations
● Basic idea:
○ How many terms does my query share with this document?
○ Are these terms important for relevance?

TF-IDF recap
● TF-IDF is very popular and empirically works well
○ It is easy to understand and very efficient
● But it has limitations
○ TF is unbounded, while intuitively should saturate over term
○ Does not encourage full query match
○ Does not account for document length (TF is still absolute)
○ Does not account for word ordering

--- PAGE 84 ---
Intermediate summary
● Sparse retrievers are 
○ Very efficient to compute
○ Good when lexical match plays a key role, good at OOD generalization
○ Do not take context into account, have simple failure cases (e.g., synonyms)
● Re-ranking is expensive, but very useful
○ Bounded by recall of base retriever
● Dual-encoder dense retrievers need supervised data to train
○ Compute-intensive AoT, but efficient at inference time
○ Generalize to other domains poorly
○ Lack fine-grained resolution (especially for long documents)
○ Often benefit from a hybrid approach
● Dense token-based retrievers are expensive at inference time, but generally work very well 84
