● Recurrent Neural Network: A family of neural networks that:

RNN with memory
● We keep both a hidden state       and a cell state 
○ Both are vectors of dimension n
○ The cell stores long-term information
○ The RNN can read, erase, and write information from the cell
■ The cell is conceptual rather like RAM in a computer
● “Long Short Term Memory” (LSTM) was introduced by Hochreiter and 
Schmidhuber in 1997 and are still in use!
○ The key idea is gating, namely, one set of weights controls the 
information flow from different sources

LSTMs summary (1/2)
● What we saw is Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)
○ Introduced in 2014 as a simplification of LSTMs by Cho et al., 2014
○ LSTMs have an explicit internal memory (“cell”) in addition to the hidden 
state that is used to produce the output
○ LSTMs use three gates (input, output, forget) instead of two
○ GRUs are easier to train and faster to run, but not be as effective as 
storing and accessing long-term dependencies.
●  LSTMs mostly fix the vanishing gradient problem and allow the RNN to 
preserve information over many timesteps
○ The gates allow cell information to be preserved indefinitely
○ It’s hard for a vanilla RNN to learn a matrix W that preserves information
○ In practice, you get about 100 timesteps rather than about 7
○ Vanishing/exploding gradients can also occur in feed-forward networks. 
One common mitigation approach is residual connections

--- PAGE 63 ---
LSTMs summary (2/2)
● They were widely and successfully used for years
● State of the art results in 2013-2015 in many tasks (handwriting recognition, 
speech recognition, machine translation, parsing, and image captioning, as well 
as language models)
● They were dominant until the early 2020s and now mostly replaced by 
Transformers
● Gating is still used in modern transformers!
● There are many tricks to improve their usefulness
Source: https://engineering.fb.com/2016/10/25/ml-applications/building-an-efficient-neural-language-model-over-a-billion-words/ Current SOTA is 20.1

Conclusion
● Language modeling is a fundamental NLP task used in machine translation, 
spelling correction, speech recognition, etc.
○ Traditional models use n-gram counts and smoothing
○ Feed-forward take into account word similarity to generalize better
○ Recurrent models can learn to exploit long-range interactions
○ Neural models dramatically reduced perplexity
● Recurrent networks were widely used until recently
○ Still in production for several tasks (e.g., time-series analysis)
○ In an alternative history, they may have stayed relevant
○ Recent works use RNNs for efficient (very) long sequences processing
● We saw: Encoder-decoder architecture, classification/generation, bi-directional 
deep LSTMs, contextualized embeddings, attention
