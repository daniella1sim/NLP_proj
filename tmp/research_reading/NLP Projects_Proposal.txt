1. Research Question and Motivation
LLMs perform ICL largely viaInduction Heads[Olsson et al., 2022]. Recent work identifiedSemantic Induction Heads
(SIHs) generalizing beyond exact lexical matches [Ren et al., 2024], yet focused on single-hop relations (A→B).
Real-world reasoning, however, requires multi-hop logic (e.g.,A⊂BandB⊂C⇒A⊂C).
Research Question:How do LLMs mechanically process multi-hop semantic relations? Are these relations resolved
by isolated SIHs, or do they require distributed Semantic Induction Circuits (SICs) spanning multiple layers?
Motivation:As LLMs become an integral part of daily applications, ensuring their reliability requires mechanistic
interpretability of their complex, inference-time reasoning. Since real-world language inherently relies on multi-hop
logic rather than sterile single-step associations, discovering distributed Semantic Induction Circuits (SICs) is essential
to demystify the true mechanical ”thought process” driving everyday AI interactions.
2. Methodology
We will use a top-down reverse-engineering approach to isolate these circuits:
•Models & Tooling:We will analyze model suites such asPythia,OLMo, orLMEnt. These suites are essen-
tial because they provide access to intermediate training checkpoints and their exact pre-training data distribu-
tions, allowing us to track the developmental dynamics of the circuits. Interventions will be implemented via
TransformerLens.
•Circuit Extraction & Evaluation:Instead of analyzing isolated heads, we aim to map full causal pathways.
We will useActivation PatchingandPath Patchingto trace the flow of information across layers, identifying the
sequence of heads forming the SIC. Crucially, to formally evaluate this, we will generalize theRelation Index
(RI)metric [Ren et al., 2024] originally used for single SIHs into a metric capable of capturing and evaluating
distributed multi-head circuits.
3. Evaluation of Success
Success will be evaluated quantitatively via few-shot ICL prompts. We define the isolated SIC as a sparse subgraph and
measure itsFaithfulness. This is done by comparing the full model’s probability for the correct multi-hop answer against
the probability when only the SIC is active. Recovering a significant portion (will be defined in the project) of the baseline
performance using only a sparse subset of heads will provide strong evidence for the existence and functional role of this
distributed circuit.
4. Assumptions and Requirements
•Compute:Standard GPU access via thestudentkillablepartition on the TAU Slurm cluster is fully sufficient
for inference and activation patching on models under 1B parameters.
•Data:We will synthetically generate controlled multi-hop ICL datasets using templates frombAbIandCLUTRR.
Replacing real nouns with fictitious entities ensures the model relies purely on in-context reasoning rather than
parametric memory.
•Codebase:We will rely on open-source libraries, specificallyPyTorchandTransformerLens.
