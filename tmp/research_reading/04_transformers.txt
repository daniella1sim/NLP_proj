Residual connections
● Problem: In deep-learning neural networks, gradients may vanish or 
explode during training.
○ Additionally, some functions H(x) are hard to learn
● Solution: Residual connections (a.k.a skip connections)
○ Instead of learning H(x) directly, learn F(x) = H(x) - x
○ Learn small modifications on x
○ Gradients propagate freely
● Residual connections allow training very deep 
neural networks
He et al., 2015 - https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.03385.pdf

--- PAGE 51 ---
Layer normalization
● When training NNs, covariate shifts are a big problem
○ For example, in convolutional neural networks, batch normalization is 
often used to make sure each batch has the same mean and variance
○ But what is a batch in recurrent networks?
● In RNNs and transformers, long sequences can cause significant shifts in 
output distribution between tokens, making the training unstable (each token 
is processed in the same way but may vary significantly in statistics)
● Layer normalization normalizes each of the inputs in the batch independently 
across all features
○ The output of each sublayer is LayerNorm(x+SubLayer(x))
○ Nowadays some transformers perform Pre-LN rather than Post-LN
Ba et al., 2016 - https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06450 
Images from https://www.pinecone.io/learn/batch-layer-normalization

--- PAGE 68 ---
The transformer encoder
Vaswani et al., 2017
● Sequence to sequence model
○ Output length is equal to the input length
○ The representation dimension at each sublayer is 
the same as the embedding dimension
● A bi-directional contextual encoder
● Fully parallelizable (across input tokens)
● Agnostic to the input length (given appropriate 
positional embeddings)
● Quadratic complexity in input length due to 
self-attention
● Transformer blocks are stacked vertically
● Increasing the representation dimension (width) 
generally improves performance

Cross attention
● We’ve seen self-attention for 
the encoder (bi-directional)
● We’ve seen masked self-attention for the 
decoder (uni-directional)
● A natural extension is cross-attention, 
allowing a decoder block to attend to 
representations of the encoder, to 
perform conditional generation

--- PAGE 94 ---
Cross attention details
● In self-attention, keys, queries, and 
values come from the same source.
● In cross-attention, things look more 
like what we saw last time.
● Namely, the keys and values come 
from the encoder, and the query 
comes from the decoder (no masking 
is needed)

Conclusion
● Deep contextualized representations replaced word embeddings
● Models are trained via a two-step process – pre-training on lots of data, 
then fine-tuning for specific tasks and/or for alignment/instruction following – 
more on that later in the course
● We moved from task-specific architectures into “adjustable” general 
language models
● Transformers broke some usability threshold and NLP has made it into the 
industry and our daily lives
● But they may not be the end of the road
