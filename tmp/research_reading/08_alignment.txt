Emergent abilities of large language models – GPT
● In 2018, OpenAI released the first GPT model
○ 117M parameters, Transformer-based, 12 layers decoder-only model
○ Trained on 7000 books (<5Gb of text)
● Showed that language modelling at scale can be an effective pretraining 
technique for downstream tasks like question answering and common 
sense reasoning. .
“We demonstrate that large gains on these tasks can be realized by generative 
pre-training of a language model on a diverse corpus of unlabeled text, 
followed by discriminative fine-tuning on each specific task.”
[Radford et al, 2018]

Limitations of instruction tuning
● It’s expensive to collect ground-truth data for tasks.
● Tasks like creative generation have no right answer.
○ Write me a story about a dog and her pet grasshopper.
● Language modeling penalizes all token-level mistakes equally, but some 
errors are worse than others.
● Even with instruction finetuning, there a mismatch between the LM 
objective and the objective of “satisfy human preferences”
● The model never learns what it really needs to avoid!

--- PAGE 61 ---
Intermediate recap
● Pre-training + fine-tuning
○ Works well for well defined tasks, even with smaller models
○ Easier to train, evaluate and control
○ Expensive (collect data, design architecture, deploy multiple models)
○ Not general purpose!
● Zero-Shot (ZS) and Few-Shot (FS) In-Context Learning
○ No finetuning needed, prompt engineering (e.g. CoT) can improve performance 
○ Limits to what you can fit in context
○ Complex tasks will probably need gradient steps
● Instruction finetuning
○ Simple and straightforward, generalize to unseen tasks
○ Collecting demonstrations for so many tasks is expensive
○ Mismatch between LM objective and human preferences
● Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

Limitations of RL
● RL is very tricky to optimize
● Human preferences are unreliable
○ ”Reward hacking” is a common problem in RL
○ Chatbots are rewarded to produce responses that seem 
authoritative and helpful, regardless of truth
■ This can result in making up facts + hallucinations
○ Models of human preferences are even more unreliable!
134

--- PAGE 135 ---
Challenges in LLM Alignment
● Many different, sometimes conflicting, often not well-defined goals
○ Whose values do we impose on the LLM?
○ Many, many gray areas
● Behavior “in the wild” remains unexpected and uncontrollable
○ Alignment methods are never (and currently, far from) perfect
○ Suppression of undesired behaviors might be “shallow”
○ Behaviors can re-surface unintentionally 
○ Behaviors can be induced adversarially by attackers (“jailbreaking”)
